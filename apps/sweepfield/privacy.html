Sweepfield Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 19, 2026

Sweepfield is a minesweeper game where every cell you open lets a flower bloom, and every cleared board adds to your bouquet. This page explains what stays on your iPhone or iPad, what the ads may use, and in what order you are asked.

What stays on your device

Your flower collection and almanac, your wins and records for each difficulty, the last difficulty you played, sound and haptics settings, and whether you bought "Remove Ads" are kept only on your device. Sweepfield has no account and no server of its own, so none of this is sent to us. Deleting the app deletes all of it.

Ads (Google AdMob)

Sweepfield shows a banner while you play, an occasional full-screen ad between games (none in the first 72 hours after you first open the app), and a short ad only when you choose one for hints or to undo a mine. To show and measure these ads, Google may use:

  • The advertising identifier, only if you allow tracking
  • IP address and approximate location
  • How you interact with the ads

See how Google uses information from apps that use its services and the Google Privacy Policy.

The order of your choices

In the EEA, the UK, Switzerland and other places where consent is required, Google's consent form comes first, and no ad loads until it is answered. If you decline there, Sweepfield does not ask for tracking permission and shows only ads that do not use tracking. Elsewhere, iOS asks whether Sweepfield may track you across other companies' apps and websites, and ads start only after you answer. You can change the consent choice any time from Settings > Privacy Settings in the app, and the tracking choice in your device's Settings.

Purchases

"Remove Ads" is a single one-time purchase handled entirely by Apple, and it hides the banner and the full-screen ads. We never see your payment details. Hints and undoing a mine are never sold; the optional ads are the only way to get them.

Children

Sweepfield is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them.

Changes
